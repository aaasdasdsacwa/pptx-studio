# Adapt to the presentation purpose

Consult the relevant section when choosing a storyline or evidence standard. These purpose families are examples, not supported/unsupported categories, fixed slide sequences, or a required selection step. Combine them when the brief spans several purposes. For an unlisted request, reason from the audience's intended outcome, available material and viewing conditions.

## Business reporting and decisions

For status reports, retrospectives, operating reviews and strategic decisions, make the decision or reporting question clear. Ground results in actual periods, targets, baselines and sources. Separate observations, explanations, risks and proposals. Use the detail needed for the decision rather than repeating a generic corporate introduction. Reading versions may retain detailed tables; spoken versions may need an overview and supporting appendix.

## Product, sales and proposals

Start from the audience's use case and the decision they need to make. Explain relevant capability through a user journey, demonstration, comparison or scenario. Use real screenshots for implemented behavior and identify proposed features as proposals. A sales deck, procurement response, investor pitch and product onboarding require different evidence and terminology; do not treat them as one marketing outline. Use supported commercial data and commitments only.

## Teaching and training

Define what learners should be able to explain or do. Sequence prerequisites, explanation, examples, practice and feedback as the subject requires. A training deck may legitimately contain dense procedures or reference material. Choose diagrams, worked problems, screenshots, demonstrations or photographs by instructional value; do not impose a keynote's sparse density or a sales narrative. Preserve correct answers and necessary conditions, optionally in notes.

## Research, academic talks and defenses

Anchor the deck in the actual research question, related context, methods, evidence, interpretation and limitations. Adjust depth for the committee, specialist audience or public. Preserve citations, experimental conditions and uncertainty. A proposal, literature seminar and completed-study defense require different treatment; a proposed experiment is not a measured result. Keep figures legible and attribute them appropriately.

## Technical explanations and implementation

Match depth to the audience's prior knowledge. Use architecture for component relationships, a sequence diagram for interactions, a process for ordered steps, or a worked example for mechanisms. Distinguish a conceptual design from a deployed system. Include operational assumptions or tradeoffs when they matter. A technical topic does not automatically require blue colors, robot icons or a layered architecture slide.

## Data analysis and quantitative reviews

Begin with the analytical question. Preserve actual sources, denominators, units, time windows, uncertainty and calculation meaning. Choose charts for the relationship being shown; a table is useful when exact values matter. Treat correlation, causation, forecasts and scenarios distinctly. A key finding can lead the story when supported; caveats affecting its meaning remain visible or easily available.

## Public talks, keynotes and storytelling

Organize around the audience's experience of the ideas: a narrative, demonstration, argument, sequence of scenes or other suitable progression. Use images, voice and rhythm when they help; not every talk needs an artificial hook or surprise. Keep factual claims sourced and fictional scenes clearly fictional. Adapt page density to live delivery and accessibility needs. Speaker notes can support timing and transitions without adding visible text.

## Brand, creative work and portfolios

Let the work and intended identity guide the visual direction. A portfolio can show a brief, constraints, process, outcome and the person's actual contribution; an exhibition or cultural presentation can use a visual sequence. Photography, illustration, large typography and restrained text can all be appropriate. Honor supplied artwork and attribution. Preserve readability and factual accuracy without forcing corporate-report conventions.

## Events, personal and community presentations

Use the actual occasion, participants, tone and available material. An event program, community proposal, travel story or personal retrospective can follow a schedule, practical guidance, visual memories or a narrative. Protect requested privacy, use only authorized personal assets, and keep names/dates accurate. Do not add business metrics or product claims to make the deck look professional.

## Mixed and new purposes

A scientific outreach class combines research accuracy and teaching. An internal product training combines product evidence and practice. A cultural project proposal combines narrative and a funding decision. Choose useful elements from each, omit irrelevant ones, and follow the user's explicit priorities when they conflict. The family name is a planning aid; it does not determine the palette, page count, degree of illustration or deliverable format.
