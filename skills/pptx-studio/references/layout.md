# Layout and relationships

Choose the representation from the relationship being explained, then adapt it to the source's visual language.

The examples below and the linked recipes are a starting set, not a closed vocabulary. A lesson, photographic story, keynote, financial review and portfolio can require very different page structures. Design another composition when the material calls for it.

When constructing or substantially rearranging a timeline, comparison, process or architecture page, read [layout-recipes.md](layout-recipes.md) for adjustable proportions, text-capacity decisions and worked content examples.

- Evolution: a timeline or staged path. Use actual dates only when provided or sourced; otherwise label stages.
- Ordered work: a process or gates with readable directions and labels.
- Feedback: a cycle that includes continuation and termination conditions where relevant.
- Comparison: aligned columns or a table with common row criteria and consistent units.
- Responsibility: a swimlane or layered architecture with named boundaries.
- Entity relationships: a graph with meaningful edges and directions.
- Spatial context: a map or annotated image with clear locations and scale when needed.
- Proof of a feature: a cropped screenshot and a focused callout.

Give the page an intentional starting point and a dominant message. Align peer objects; give related objects proximity and unrelated groups space. Use cards for real groups, not as the default wrapper around every sentence.

Follow a supplied reference by extracting its reusable choices: title hierarchy, margins, typography, colors, line treatment, and diagram conventions. Preserve requested wording during layout-only work. Repair space conflicts by changing the arrangement or splitting permitted content before continually shrinking text.

For limited-scope edits, reuse good elements already on the slide. Check both local alignment and balance with untouched sections.

Completion: the visual relationship agrees with the argument, connectors communicate actual relationships, and the selected layout survives final-file rendering at the intended viewing size.
