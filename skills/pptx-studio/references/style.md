# Visual style

Inspect the user's reference/template before choosing a style. Identify its typography hierarchy, spacing, palette, imagery, diagram language, and brand marks. Apply those choices to the selected scope while preserving reference features the user explicitly wants.

Without a reference, infer a coherent direction from audience and purpose; ask for a preference only when the choice materially affects the result. Aesthetic variety can come from content-appropriate compositions rather than mixing unrelated icon and illustration families.

Treat style and purpose as independent dimensions. A business deck may use editorial typography, a classroom deck may use illustration, and an academic deck may use large photographic figures. Formal, editorial, Swiss, photographic, illustrated, expressive and minimal approaches are examples, not mandatory presets. Respect user colors and branding. Select reusable typography, color, spacing and asset treatment before composing pages; vary page structures according to their role.

Use hierarchy and whitespace to establish a focal point. Keep text/background contrast sufficient and retain shape/label distinctions alongside colors. For a spoken presentation, larger type and simpler pages are a practical starting point; verify actual viewing distance instead of enforcing a universal font-size formula.

Separate navigational chapter labels from the page's main message. Use decorative lines and shadows sparingly. Branding remains accurate; preserve logos and do not redraw a brand mark as if it were official.

Completion: the edited pages read as a coherent presentation, the chosen reference is recognizable where applicable, and style changes do not obscure meaning or evidence.
